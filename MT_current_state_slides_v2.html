A briefing for linguists

Machine translation, now

Meaning, calque, and what a single score conceals.

Drawn from the November 2026 synthesis Translation Technology and Quality, with the five workspace papers on LLM-era MT, human-centered use, literality, idiom quality estimation, and literary evaluation.

Open by stating the room: this is not a product tour. The question is whether current MT, including prompted large language models, can be trusted as a theory of meaning transfer — and how linguists should rate that transfer.

Stake

A fluent sentence is not, by that fact, a translation.

Smooth target-language form can hide mistranslation, omission, or a calque that never did the source’s job.

Give a one-line example from the audience’s language pair if you have one: an idiom rendered word-for-word that still “reads well”. Then: this is why a linguist’s object of study is not n-gram overlap.

Quality is not one property

Three aims, often in tension

Meaning

Preserve what the source is doing: referents, pragmatic force, information structure.

Naturalness

Target-language grammar, collocation, idiom, register — not source-shaped syntax.

Fitness

Audience and use: gist, publication, literature, medicine, law. Error tolerance is not universal.

Flamich et al. (2025) formalize an accuracy–naturalness frontier: blending the two into one number conceals the tradeoff. Do not present their metric as a law of translation; present the distinction.

Technology

A history of tradeoffs, not of error-free arrival

After Ataman et al., 2025. Dedicated NMT can still win on control, terminology, and cost.

Stress: an LLM is a generative sequence model prompted to translate. It is not a new account of equivalence. Specialized MT remains relevant where terminology and consistency are the product.

Meaning without calque

The test is what the source is doing — not whether the target mirrors its form.

Avoiding calque is not a ban on close translation. It is a ban on source-shaped wording that misstates sense or fails in the target context.

Nida: interpretation in cultural context, not word-for-word substitution. Vinay and Darbelnet’s procedures are still the right metalanguage for talking to linguists about what the machine did not do.

Procedures that keep meaning

Depart from form on purpose

A target-only fluency pass misses meaning errors. A source-only literalness pass punishes valid reformulation.

Walk one idiom through equivalence. Then: machines can look less literal without having chosen the right function. Raunak et al. already warned that “less literal” ≠ “idiom solved”.

Evidence · Marmonier, Bawden, Sagot 2026

Human translations remain less literal

Direct translation

Across 54 pairs, human references stay significantly less literal than NMT and most LLMs. Recent models narrow the gap; they do not close it.

Self-revision

When prompted to revise themselves, tested LLMs deliteralize monotonically — the deliteralization hypothesis appears in generation, not only in human drafting.

The Synthetic Literality Index is a heuristic bundle, English-source heavy in related work, not a full quality model. Quote the finding, then the limit. Preprint, under review.

Post-editing

LLMs invert the triggers of human revisers.

Human post-editors tend to repair highly literal drafts. Tested LLMs tolerate literal drafts and instead rewrite idiomatic human wording — then, once they edit, they deliteralize indiscriminately.

This is the linguistically sharp finding: the model can be made to sound less literal without sharing the diagnostic of a translator. It is not “the model has learned modulation”.

MQM is a framework, not one universal score. Freitag et al.: professional, context-aware MQM rankings differ from crowd scores. Zhang et al.: with student annotators, MQM misjudges a large share of published literary translations as no better than MT.

Papineni et al., 2002

BLEU was proposed as a cheap aid to human evaluation — not as a test of sense.

A translation may keep the meaning with different wording and still lose on n-gram overlap. Learned metrics track human judgments more closely on many benchmarks; none in a major comparison matched humans perfectly.

If someone in the room still reports “the BLEU is high, so the translation is good”, this is the slide. COMET predicts human-derived signals; it remains an estimate.

Idioms · Marmonier, Sagot, Bawden 2026

Many quality estimators prefer the calque

Source idiom: kick the bucket

Calque

Copy the form. Formal correspondence: kick the bucket.

Literal rendering of an idiom. Often scored higher by older metrics and smaller LLM judges.

Équivalence

Keep the function. A different expression for the same effect: die.

Idiomatic rendering. Only a small subset of tested systems consistently preferred this across nine target languages.

English-source, curated pairs. The lesson is not “all QE is useless”. The lesson is: a higher automated score need not reward the intended meaning.

This is the slide that earns the talk for phraseologists. Connect to Vinay’s équivalence: the metric is often scoring formal correspondence, which is exactly the error.

Fluency as camouflage

Detached, source-unrelated output is more common where resources are thin.

Hallucination studies on multilingual MT find higher detected rates in low-resource directions. Smooth prose is not a fidelity certificate. Language-resource level is a risk variable.

Guerreiro et al., TACL 2023. Rates depend on detector and model. For linguists working on lesser-resourced languages: do not import English-pair confidence.

Context

Sentence scores can miss the text

Pronouns, terminology, cohesion, and voice live above the sentence. In Chinese–English news, professional raters who saw whole documents preferred human translation on adequacy where sentence-level scores had not.

Paragraph context helps some LLM literary translation, but omissions and critical errors remain (Karpinska and Iyyer, 2023).

Läubli, Sennrich and Volk: the “human parity” claim was an evaluation design claim as much as a systems claim. Linguists already knew that. Say so.

Literary MT · Zhang, Zhao, Eger

Who judges, and with which scheme, is part of the result

Complex MQM + students: nearly 60% of published human translations misread as equal or inferior to MT. Simpler best–worst scaling identifies human translations at 80–100%. Automatic metrics: at most about 20%. Published human literary translations still outperform the tested LLMs, which stay more literal and less diverse.

Do not weaponize this against MQM in technical translation. The point is genre-sensitive evaluation: a taxonomy built for localization error-spotting is a poor instrument for literary choice.

Human–AI work

Post-editing is a hypothesis, not a saving

Gains vary by study, error type, language pair, and editor expertise. Idiom and word-order damage can cost more than drafting. One student English–Chinese study found less pausing in PE, yet raters still preferred from-scratch work more often.

Carpuat et al.: keep the human as agent in the loop; evaluate MT inside real communicative tasks, not only on a leaderboard.

O’Brien against antagonistic dualisms: CAT, MT, and the translator are not a morality play. Accountability for the final text remains a professional, not a model, property.

A compact set for linguists

Report six things, separately

1 · Meaning

Meaning preservation — source-visible adequacy plus major MQM meaning errors.

2 · Naturalness

Naturalness and idiomaticity — a separate target-language judgment.

3 · Document

Document context — referents, terms, discourse, voice.

4 · Task fit

Task fit — including risk review for medical, legal, and public text.

5 · Workflow

Workflow cost — PE effort, kept apart from quality.

6 · Auto scores

Automatic scores — BLEU, ChrF, COMET, QE as screening, never as the verdict.

A system that is accurate but stiff is not the same object as one that reads well and omits. If the room leaves with only this list, the talk has succeeded.

Limits

What this briefing does not claim

It does not claim that MT is useless, that LLMs have solved idioms, or that one 2026 index is a theory of translation. Several cited studies are English-source, small-sample, or still under review. High-resource news is not literature, and literature is not a consent form.

Honesty about preprints (Mar26, Mar26c) protects the speaker. The directional findings are still the right ones to teach: literality, metric bias, evaluation design.

Close

Keep automated scores for screening. Keep linguists for meaning, idiom, and use.

The current state of machine translation is high fluency, uneven fidelity, and evaluation that still rewards the calque unless someone competent looks.
